Layers

Cut off

Only the part of the model between the two cuts is printed. Cut the top where it stops being flat (a dome or a point ends in a few tiny, wobbly last layers that don't hold their shape in chocolate), or the bottom of a round model so it stands on a flat first layer. The model's preview on the Line step fades the parts cut off and outlines the cuts; the Height above is the part that's left. Turning the model clears the cuts.

A model prints its outside contour only: the inside surface of a hollow model (a cup's inner wall) and any hole are not traced. Walls adds lines inside the outside contour, one spacing apart; how much of the inside gets chocolate is up to Base and Infill.

A bead can only reach a little past the layer under it (Overhang per layer, in Fine-tuning) before it has nothing to sit on. One continuous line can't print a separate support, so the support is the print's own wall, widened from the plate up, and it stays on the piece. Flared skirt: the support pulls back towards the model one overhang step a layer, from its far edge only, so it fans out under a cap and braces an arm back into the model at that slope (least chocolate). Thin wall: a wall the width of one narrow loop runs from the plate up under each overhang, into the model, and flares out near the top to hold the whole overhang (a standing r keeps its arm, with a thin wall under it; on a round model it is the same as the skirt). Each layer stays one loop. The ready list says how far the model steps out with Support off.

Fills every area the line encloses with more of the same line, still one continuous line. Density 100% puts the rows a line width apart, touching (solid chocolate); 50% two line widths apart; 0% leaves the inside open. Working out a fill can take a few seconds on a big drawing.

0% · hollow
fill all

Fills the inside of the layers above the base, as part of the same line: 0% leaves them hollow (walls only), 20–30% is a light support, 100% is solid.

Test print

Numbered strips side by side, each the same small zigzag a few layers tall, with one setting stepped from strip to strip. A strip's number is the count of teeth in the comb printed in front of it, and strip 1 is on the left. The layers go round every strip before stepping up, so each has time to set. Print it, pick the best strip, and Use it.

    Everything starts here, one thing at a time: a picture to trace, typed text, a geometric pattern or a 3D model. Each keeps its own settings when you switch. Drop a file anywhere on the page: an image becomes the picture, an STL the model, a saved design opens.

    Picture

    Photo

    A photo of a page is straightened (its four corners pulled to a rectangle) and cleaned for the line. Drawing: the paper's light is evened out (shadows and a lamp's falloff go), the paper made white and the ink black; faint pencil is darkened. Printed: the same, gentler, keeping the greys (try Grey levels, below). Object: no page; its colours are balanced and stretched (tap its background in Image prep to remove it). As taken: only straightened. The photo itself stays with the picture until another picture is used; a saved design keeps the cleaned picture.

    0°

    Turns the picture before anything else: a quarter turn at a time, or a few degrees to straighten a photo (the corners that come in are white, the paper). To crop, use Select area on the Line step: the trace keeps to the selection and gets the full resolution there.

    A photo taken with the camera, a sample, an image of yours (or drop one anywhere on the page) or a sketch drawn on the preview. A photo of a page is straightened and cleaned up for the line (Photo, below); Clean up as a photo does the same to an image you chose. To edit a traced line by hand, export it as SVG from the Plot step, fix it in a drawing app (Illustrator, Affinity, Inkscape) and drop it back in here.

    Text

    1 of 5 lines

    Single-stroke lettering: every letter is drawn with the same stroke at the same height. Even gaps keep the closest point between neighbouring letters at exactly the letter gap.

    Pattern

    Picture

    Geometric art drawn as exact lines, then traced. The first group are patterns of their own; the second draw a picture's light and dark (the Picture row picks it), still as one line, so they print in chocolate. Labyrinth, Moore loop and Double spiral are one closed line already, and Hilbert, Gosper, Whirl and Waves one open line, with an even gap between neighbouring lines: they print without doubled strokes (the Line card's Closed loop or Open is set to suit). Width sets the print's size in mm, and says how far apart its closest lines come against the needle's line spacing; Fit the needle picks the width where they are exactly that. Spirograph, Lissajous and Rose are one line that crosses itself: the trace follows their exact line, and with Allow overlaps (the Line step's Line card) the needle prints over each crossing; without it every crossing is undone. Truchet tiles are many pieces: those suit the pen plotter. Every style has its own settings below the style; Shuffle gives the random ones another version.

    3D model

    Which way up it prints

    Big models (hundreds of thousands of triangles) load, turn and slice slowly. Drag the preview to look around it. The Print step slices it into layers and fits each one with a Fourier series; set its height there. The Line step's line spacing is the wall's.

    Line drawing of this viewfolds over 20°

    Outlines and folds as seen from the preview's angle, hidden lines removed (like plotter.vision). At the far left every edge between faces that aren't flat together is drawn, a wireframe (the plotter.vision look); further right only sharper folds and the outline. Export SVG saves the drawing itself as vectors (every visible edge, at the model's size). Draw this view makes it the picture to trace, for a printer or the Plot step; a dense wireframe is more than one line can follow, so keep the angle up for tracing. Pick 3D model again to change the angle. Drawing or exporting a view takes a few seconds; a wireframe (far left) takes longest.

    Grey levels off

    off

    Fills are drawn with Separate strokes (the switch at the top of this step); with one continuous line only the traced edges are plotted.

    The picture as 2 to 6 flat greys, found from its own greys (darkest first). Each grey's edges can be traced (Trace) or left out (Skip): the line follows the edges between the greys you trace, like an outline of each block. Making it for a pen plotter, a grey can also be filled (Fill), or traced and filled (Both): its blocks are hatched, cross-hatched, ringed or filled with the Pattern card's pattern, with its own pen (its own layer in the SVG); a filled grey that isn't traced still stops its fill at its edges. Smooth blocks gives every pixel the grey most of its neighbours have, which removes specks and rounds the blocks' corners so their edges trace clean. With grey levels on, the Tracing card's trace style and ink threshold are not used. The still-life sample (Art step, Picture) shows them well.

    The line

    Trace is off (the switch under the preview):

    Everything the Line step does follows this switch. One continuous line is the Fourier trace, the only line a printer can make, and a plotter can draw it without lifting the pen. Separate strokes traces the art into strokes the pen draws one by one, lifting between them: closer to the drawing, nothing joined or doubled. With Trace off (the switch under the preview) nothing is traced: a pattern's or the text's exact lines go to the page, and a picture shows as it is (it has no lines until it is traced), so nothing re-traces while you adjust the art.

    Tracing

    Grey levels are on (the card below): the line follows the edges between the greys, so the trace style and the ink threshold aren't used.

    Trace style

    Line

    Spacing

    The gap is the space left between neighbouring beads, edge to edge. The line keeps its parts at least line width + gap apart, centre to centre, everywhere: the doubled strands of a stroke, the loops at stroke ends, and the smallest size the print can shrink to (it grows rather than letting lines overlap). Only where strokes meet at a junction may they touch, so they join. For chocolate, the line width is the needle and the flow is set for a bead exactly that wide and one layer tall, so a gap of 0 puts beads side by side, just touching, without overlapping. A little gap (0.5 mm to start) leaves room for chocolate that spreads before it sets: raise it if beads merge, lower it if you want them to touch. Base and Infill at 100% density work the same way (rows one line width apart). For a pen drawing, the line width is the pen and the gap is only how much white you want between strokes.

    Path

    The line can't stop or go back over itself, so it comes back from every stroke end as a narrow loop: out on one side, round a small loop, back on the other, one line spacing apart. A short connector, a straight strand across to a nearby loose end, can replace the way back (the line still doesn't stop).

    off

    Rounds sharp corners in the drawing to at least this radius, so the needle never turns tighter. Stroke-end loops, junctions and connectors keep their shape.

    Removing a crossing reverses a short stretch of the line so the strands turn back instead of crossing. "Remove all" also keeps strands slightly apart; the gap between lines sets the real gap.

    Still one unbroken line, but it may cross itself where the drawing does (a spirograph, a Lissajous figure, a rose): the needle prints straight over the line already there. Round each crossing the strands are let come closer than the spacing, as far as two lines crossing at that angle need; everywhere else the spacing is kept as usual. Off, every crossing is undone as set above. Painted crossing areas work either way.

    The page

    For a pen plotter (the AxiDraw, through Saxi). What goes on the page is set by the Line step: one continuous line (the traced drawing, one stroke per line, no doubled strands or stroke-end loops), separate strokes (the pen lifting between them), or a pattern or text as drawn. The drawing fills the page inside the margins.

      The mathFormula, terms and error score

      The series

      x(t)=x0+∑kak⁢cos(2πkt+φk)y(t)=y0+∑kbk⁢cos(2πkt+ψk)z(t)=00≤t<1

      Each axis is its own series. A term is one axis at one frequency: a circle of radius ak (or bk) turning k times while t runs from 0 to 1, starting at angle φk (or ψk). Chain the X circles and the tip's left–right position is x; chain the Y circles and the tip's height is y; the pen is where the two meet. Terms are ordered by size across both axes, so the slider keeps the strongest first. z is the height above the print surface: 0 for a flat print, and later the climb of a continuous spiral.
